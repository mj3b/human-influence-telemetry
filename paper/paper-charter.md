# Paper Charter

## Decision problem

Institutional records often show that a person appeared in an AI-mediated decision process without showing whether that person accessed relevant evidence, exercised judgment, held practical authority, corrected error, repaired harm, or changed the decision architecture.

## Research question

Can an open, machine-readable documentary method distinguish substantive human influence, ceremonial human presence, affirmative absence, and insufficient evidence while preserving explicit limits on empirical conclusions?

## Intended contribution

The paper may claim a specified documentary method, an executable contract, a complete-record conformance layer, a bounded two-scorer result for one frozen packet under contract 0.1.0, and an audited claim-to-evidence publication workflow. It may describe current-contract replication as unresolved.

The paper may not claim population reliability, causal effectiveness, legal conformity, current-contract replication, independent institutional adoption, or field validity.

## Audience and decision

The primary audience is researchers and practitioners evaluating human oversight in AI-mediated institutional decisions. The paper should let that reader decide whether HIT supplies a sufficiently explicit method and evidence boundary to justify independent replication or prospective testing.

## Completion rule

A submission candidate requires completed literature review, source-level verification, author approval of every mapped support judgment, regenerated figures, an independent methodological review when available, and a venue-specific reporting audit.
